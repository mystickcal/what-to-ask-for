Passkeys (WebAuthn)

Also called: WebAuthn, FIDO2, passwordless login, Face ID login, security keys

Sign-in with a key pair stored on the user's phone, laptop or password manager, unlocked with Face ID, a fingerprint or a PIN. Your server keeps only the public key, so there's no password to phish or leak.

You need it when

  • You want the strongest login without making users manage codes.
  • Phishing or password reuse is a real risk for your users.

Skip it when

  • It would be your only login method on day one. Keep email or password as a fallback while users adopt it.

Ways to build it

  1. A WebAuthn library $0

    SimpleWebAuthn (TypeScript), py_webauthn (Python) or webauthn-ruby handle the challenge and verification on both sides.

    Tradeoff: Full control and no vendor. You build the account and recovery flows.

  2. Your auth library or provider $0 to plan-based

    django-allauth, Better Auth, Clerk, Auth0, Supabase and others support passkeys as a login or second factor.

    Tradeoff: Least code. Setup and recovery look the way they decide.

Prompt for your coding agent

Replace the [bracketed] parts. Works in Claude Code, Cursor or Codex.

Add passkey sign-in to my app with [library].

Requirements:
- Signed-in users can add a passkey (named by device), list them with last-used date, and remove them.
- Login page offers "Sign in with a passkey" and uses autofill (conditional mediation) where the browser supports it.
- Generate a fresh random challenge per attempt on the server, with a short expiry, and verify origin and RP ID.
- Store credential id, public key, sign count, transports, created and last used. Never store anything that could act as a password.
- Keep email login as a fallback. Removing the last sign-in method is blocked.
- Email the user when a passkey is added or removed.
- Test on iPhone Safari, Android Chrome, and desktop with a password manager.

Acceptance checks (run each and show me the result):
1. Adding a passkey on a phone and signing in with Face ID or a fingerprint works.
2. A passkey registered for your production domain fails on a lookalike domain.
3. A replayed login response with an old challenge is rejected.
4. Users can't remove their last way to sign in.
5. The passkeys list shows each device and its last use.

If you skip it

Users get locked out when they change phones, or the flow works on one browser and silently fails on others.

People describe this as

  • “let users sign in with Face ID or a fingerprint”
  • “add passwordless login”
  • “stop phishing of user passwords”
  • “support YubiKeys”
