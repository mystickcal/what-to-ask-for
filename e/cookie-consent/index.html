Cookie consent and privacy-friendly analytics

Also called: cookie banner, GDPR consent, consent management, cookieless analytics, tracking consent

Asking before you set non-essential cookies or load trackers, where the law requires it, and not loading them until people agree. Many apps avoid most of the problem by using cookieless, privacy-focused analytics and keeping ad pixels off logged-in pages.

You need it when

  • You have visitors in the UK or EU and use analytics, ad pixels or other non-essential cookies.

Skip it when

  • You only set cookies strictly needed for the service, like login and CSRF. Check the rules for where your users are.

Ways to build it

  1. Cookieless analytics $0 self-hosted

    Umami, Plausible or GoatCounter (self-hosted or their cloud) count visits without tracking cookies.

    Tradeoff: Often no banner needed for analytics, but check the rules where your users are. Less detail than ad-tech tools.

  2. A small consent banner you build $0

    Store the choice in a first-party cookie and only inject third-party scripts after 'Accept'. Make 'Reject' as easy as 'Accept'.

    Tradeoff: Light and fully controlled. You maintain the list of what each choice loads.

  3. A consent management platform free tiers, paid

    A hosted consent tool, or Google's consent mode alongside it if you use Google tags.

    Tradeoff: Handles many vendors and regions. Heavier pages and another vendor.

Prompt for your coding agent

Replace the [bracketed] parts. Works in Claude Code, Cursor or Codex.

Set up consent-aware analytics for this app.

Requirements:
- List every cookie and third-party script the app loads, and mark each as strictly necessary or not.
- Load nothing non-essential (analytics, ad pixels, chat widgets) until the visitor accepts. Reject must be one click and as visible as Accept.
- Store the choice in a first-party cookie, and add a link in the footer to change it later.
- Keep third-party trackers off logged-in pages and pages with personal data entirely.
- If possible, switch analytics to a cookieless tool so the banner covers less.
- Update the privacy policy to match what's actually loaded.

Acceptance checks (run each and show me the result):
1. With no choice made, the network tab shows no requests to analytics or ad domains.
2. After Reject, still none.
3. After Accept, the chosen scripts load.
4. Logged-in pages never load ad pixels, whatever the choice.

If you skip it

Complaints and fines under privacy laws, and personal data from inside your app leaking to ad networks.

How Derek runs it

Custody Journal loads third-party analytics only for signed-out visitors, only on the home, start and pricing pages, and only after the cj_analytics_consent_v1 cookie is set to granted.
