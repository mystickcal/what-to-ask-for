Video upload and streaming

Also called: video hosting, HLS, transcoding, adaptive streaming, Mux, Cloudflare Stream

Taking a video file, turning it into several sizes and qualities, and sending viewers small pieces that fit their connection. A plain MP4 link works for short clips, but long or user uploaded video needs encoding into a streaming format like HLS so it starts fast and doesn't stall.

You need it when

  • Users upload their own videos, or you host lessons, demos or recordings.
  • Videos are longer than a minute or two, or people watch on phones.
  • Some videos should only be watched by people who paid or logged in.

Skip it when

  • A few public marketing videos. An unlisted YouTube or Vimeo embed is free and good enough.

Ways to build it

  1. Video API Per minute stored and watched. Cloudflare Stream lists $5 per 1,000 minutes stored and $1 per 1,000 delivered

    Mux, Cloudflare Stream or Bunny Stream take the upload, encode it, and give you a playback ID and a player.

    Tradeoff: Quickest to do well. Costs grow with every minute watched, and your videos live with them.

  2. Encode it yourself $0 plus servers and bandwidth

    A background job runs ffmpeg to make HLS renditions, stores them in object storage and serves them through a CDN.

    Tradeoff: Cheapest at scale and fully yours. Encoding is slow and heavy, and you own every playback bug.

  3. Embed from a video site $0

    Upload to YouTube or Vimeo as unlisted and embed their player.

    Tradeoff: Free and simple. Their branding and suggestions, weak privacy, and no fit for user uploads.

Prompt for your coding agent

Replace the [bracketed] parts. Works in Claude Code, Cursor or Codex.

Add video upload and streaming.

Requirements:
- Use [Mux / Cloudflare Stream / ffmpeg to HLS on our own storage]. Upload from the browser straight to the provider or storage, not through my server.
- Accept [MP4, MOV, WebM] up to [2 GB] and [60 minutes]. Check type and size before the upload starts.
- Save a video record with owner, status (uploading, processing, ready, failed), duration and provider ID. Update it from the provider's webhook.
- Show a processing state, then the player when ready. Show a clear message if processing fails.
- Private videos use signed playback URLs that expire. Only the owner and people allowed by [rule] can get one.
- Deleting a video in the app deletes it from the provider or storage.

Acceptance checks (run each and show me the result):
1. Upload a 1 GB test video from a phone on a slow connection. It finishes, and the page shows processing, then ready.
2. Play it on a phone and a laptop. Quality changes with the connection without the video stopping for long.
3. A private video's link stops working for a logged out user or after it expires.
4. Delete a video in the app. It's gone from storage and the player too, and stops counting toward storage costs.

If you skip it

Big uploads time out on your server, viewers on phones sit through buffering, and private videos end up on a public link anyone can share.

People describe this as

  • “let users upload videos and play them back smoothly”
  • “my videos buffer and take forever to load”
  • “host course videos in my app”
