Email authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC)

Also called: email deliverability, SPF record, DKIM signing, DMARC policy, emails going to spam

Three DNS records that let inbox providers check that mail really comes from you. SPF lists which servers may send for your domain. DKIM signs each message with a key published in DNS. DMARC says what to do when those checks fail and where to send reports.

You need it when

  • As soon as your app sends any email from your own domain.
  • Gmail's sender guidelines require them, with stricter rules for bulk senders.

Skip it when

  • You don't send from your own domain at all.

Ways to build it

  1. A transactional email provider free or cheap tiers

    Postmark, Resend or Amazon SES give you the DKIM records to paste into DNS and handle sending reputation.

    Tradeoff: The easy path for password resets and receipts. Per-message cost at volume and another account.

  2. Self-hosted mail server $0 software

    Postfix with OpenDKIM on a VPS.

    Tradeoff: Hard to keep out of spam. New VPS IPs often have poor reputation, and many hosts block outbound port 25. Not a good choice for password resets.

  3. Self-hosted newsletter tool over provider SMTP $0 software

    Listmonk for lists and campaigns, sending through a provider's SMTP.

    Tradeoff: You own the list. You still need the same DNS records, and newsletters should use a separate subdomain or stream from transactional mail.

Prompt for your coding agent

Replace the [bracketed] parts. Works in Claude Code, Cursor or Codex.

Set up email authentication for [domain], sending through [provider].

Requirements:
- List the exact DNS records to add: one SPF TXT record that includes the provider, the provider's DKIM records, and a DMARC record at _dmarc starting with p=none and a rua reporting address.
- Keep transactional mail (resets, receipts) separate from marketing mail: different subdomain or provider stream.
- Make the app's From address use the authenticated domain.
- In local development, send all mail to Mailpit instead of real inboxes.
- Add List-Unsubscribe headers to marketing mail.

Acceptance checks (run each and show me the result):
1. dig TXT [domain] shows exactly one SPF record.
2. A test email to Gmail shows SPF, DKIM and DMARC as PASS under Show original.
3. A mail-tester.com run lists no authentication problems.
4. Local development never delivers to a real inbox.
5. After DMARC reports show only your real senders passing, the policy moves to p=quarantine.

If you skip it

Password resets and receipts land in spam or get rejected, and users think your app is broken.

People describe this as

  • “my password reset emails go to spam”
  • “Gmail says it can't verify the sender”
  • “set up my domain to send email”
  • “send receipts from my own domain”
