Email verification

Also called: confirm email address, verify email, email confirmation link, verified email, change email flow

Sending a one-time link or code to the address a user gives and marking it verified only when they use it. It proves they own the inbox before you send them anything important or let that address reset a password.

You need it when

  • Email is used for password reset, billing, invites or anything private.
  • Users can change their email address.

Skip it when

  • Sign-in is only by magic link or a provider that already verified the email.

Ways to build it

  1. Your auth library's verification $0

    django-allauth, Devise confirmable, Laravel MustVerifyEmail, Better Auth and Auth.js email flows.

    Tradeoff: Handled for you. Decide whether unverified users can use the app at all.

  2. Hosted auth free tiers

    Clerk, Supabase Auth, Firebase Auth and Auth0 send verification emails and expose a verified flag.

    Tradeoff: No code. Their email templates and sending domain unless you configure yours.

  3. Your own signed tokens $0

    A random token stored as a hash with user id, email and expiry, or a signed token with your framework's signer.

    Tradeoff: Simple. Remember to bind it to the exact email being verified.

Prompt for your coding agent

Replace the [bracketed] parts. Works in Claude Code, Cursor or Codex.

Add email verification at signup and on email change.

Requirements:
- After signup, send a link with a random single-use token (stored as a hash) that expires in [24 hours]. The token is tied to the user id and the exact email.
- Until verified, allow [limited use] and block [billing, invites, exports].
- Resend button with a rate limit. Old links stop working when a new one is sent.
- Email change: keep the old address active, send the link to the new one, switch only after it's clicked, and notify the old address.
- Password reset never goes to an unverified address.
- The same "check your inbox" message whether or not the address exists, so the form can't be used to find accounts.
- Clicking the link while logged out still verifies, then asks the user to log in.

Acceptance checks (run each and show me the result):
1. A link works once and fails after expiry.
2. Changing email doesn't switch until the new address confirms, and the old address gets a notice.
3. A token made for one email can't verify a different email on the same account.
4. Resend is throttled.
5. Unverified accounts can't reach the blocked features through the API.

If you skip it

Someone signs up with another person's email and later takes over their account, or your sending reputation sinks from typo addresses.

People describe this as

  • “make sure users own the email they signed up with”
  • “people sign up with fake emails”
  • “someone signed up with my email address”
  • “let users change their email safely”
