Inbound email parsing

Also called: receive email, email to app, reply by email, email webhooks, parse incoming email

Letting your app receive email. A provider accepts mail for an address or domain you own, turns each message into JSON (sender, subject, text, attachments) and posts it to a webhook in your app.

You need it when

  • Users should reply to notifications by email, forward things in, or email a unique address to add data.

Skip it when

  • You only need to read one mailbox occasionally. A shared inbox tool may be enough.

Ways to build it

  1. Your email provider's inbound webhook free tiers, then paid

    Postmark, SendGrid Inbound Parse, Mailgun Routes, Resend and Amazon SES receiving post parsed messages to your URL.

    Tradeoff: Reliable and simple. You point a subdomain's MX record at them.

  2. Cloudflare Email Routing with a Worker free

    Email Workers receive messages for your domain and can forward or post them to your app.

    Tradeoff: Free and flexible. You parse MIME yourself (postal-mime helps).

  3. Poll a mailbox over IMAP $0

    A scheduled job reads a dedicated mailbox and processes new messages.

    Tradeoff: Works with any mailbox. Slower, and fragile with folder states and logins.

Prompt for your coding agent

Replace the [bracketed] parts. Works in Claude Code, Cursor or Codex.

Let my app receive email at [reply.mydomain.com] using [provider]'s inbound webhook.

Requirements:
- Use a subdomain for MX so normal company mail is unaffected.
- Verify each webhook is from the provider (signature, basic auth or secret path) and store the raw message id so retries aren't processed twice.
- Route by address: reply+<token>@ maps to a thread with a random token, not a guessable id.
- Check the sender matches the user the token belongs to. Look at SPF and DKIM results from the provider and drop failures.
- Strip quoted replies and signatures so only the new text is saved.
- Accept attachments up to [10 MB], check type and size, and store them like other uploads.
- Process in a background job. Return 200 quickly.
- Ignore auto-replies and bounce messages (Auto-Submitted header, empty return path) to avoid mail loops.

Acceptance checks (run each and show me the result):
1. Replying to a notification adds the reply text, without the quoted history, to the right thread.
2. An email from a different sender to the same reply address is rejected.
3. An out-of-office reply doesn't create anything or trigger another email.
4. The same webhook delivered twice creates one record.
5. A 50 MB attachment is refused cleanly.

If you skip it

Anyone who learns an address can post into a user's account, or two auto-responders email each other forever.

People describe this as

  • “let users reply to a notification email and have it show up in the app”
  • “forward receipts to a special address”
  • “create a ticket when someone emails support”
  • “each user gets their own email address in my app”
